A foundation before you begin

Marital intimacy is a good gift to be learned together, not a performance to pass. Marriage never removes either person's voice or right to pause. Mutual service does not mean pressure, guilt, enduring pain, or doing something merely to avoid disappointing the other person.

Consent must be freely given, ongoing and reversible. A previous "yes" is not permanent permission. If something feels painful, frightening or unsafe, stop and seek appropriate medical, pastoral or therapeutic help.

Discuss together:

  • What if one or both of you are exhausted, nervous or not ready?
  • What would make the evening feel affectionate and meaningful without pressure?
  • How will you respond if your first experiences are awkward or do not go as planned?
  • How will you protect Peace from feeling that Stephen's past experience creates a performance standard?

4. Our communication and consent language

Each person should be able to communicate four things clearly: yes, slower, pause and stop.

5. Beliefs, preferences and non-negotiables

Privately discuss which practices each person considers acceptable, unacceptable or uncertain. Include pornography and sexualised media, privacy, sharing intimate details with others, and any practice either person regards as physically, emotionally or spiritually unsafe.

Do not negotiate a reluctant "no" into a "yes". If you disagree, record the topic as unresolved and seek wise guidance. Do not put explicit details in this form.
