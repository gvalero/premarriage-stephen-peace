Spiritual Gifts & Finances homework

Name the gift, then build on the foundation

Two parts this time. First, we finish our Spiritual Life chapter with a little gift swap — each of you naming the gifts you see in the other, then calling them out all week long. Then we open our next chapter together: finances — honestly, with no shame, and as a team.

Proverbs 10:22 The blessing of the Lord makes one rich, and He adds no sorrow to it.

Complete in order

  1. The gift swap (do it now)
  2. Call it out all week
  3. Read pages 54-59
  4. Answer the finance questions

How to complete this

  1. Pray briefly before you start.
  2. Do Part A (the gift swap) first, on your own — do not compare notes with each other yet.
  3. Then live it out: through the week, when you catch your partner living out the gift you named, say it to them in the moment.
  4. Read pages 54-59 of the premarital counselling book: Finances. If you don't have the book to hand, download the chapter as a PDF here (6 pages).
  5. Complete the finance sections separately first, then talk them through together.
  6. At the end, copy or download your answers and send them back before our next session.

We will go through your answers together at our next session, so short, honest, specific answers are better than long ones. Your answers stay in this browser only - nothing is uploaded automatically.

1. Who is completing this?

Part A — The Gift Swap (finishing Spiritual Life)

Last session we ran out of time to do this together, so let's finish it now. The idea is simple: each of you, on your own, names the spiritual gift(s) you see in your partner. Not yourself — your partner. This is a gift you are giving them: the gift of being seen.

Examples of spiritual gifts & qualities (from 1 Corinthians 12 and beyond) — pick from these or use your own words:
wisdom · knowledge · faith · healing · prophecy (speaking truth that stirs people) · discernment · teaching · leadership · administration / organising · serving · helping · encouragement · hospitality · generosity · mercy · intercession (prayer) · evangelism · worship / music · perseverance · peacemaking.

Part B — The Reading: Finances (book pp. 54-59)

Read pp. 54-59 of the book - Finances. If you don't have the book, the chapter is here: download the 6-page PDF.

The chapter covers four things: unity in finances (seeing money as "ours", not "yours/mine"), tithing, spending habits, and a budget. The questions below mirror the ones in the book. There is no shame here - "we haven't talked about this yet" is a completely valid answer, and naming it now is exactly the point.

2. Unity in finances

Unity grows when money is "ours" and there's no secrecy. These are honest, on-the-table questions - answer for yourself first, then you'll talk them through.

4. Spending habits

As singles you each decided how, when, and on what you spent. Now there are two of you. Differences here are normal - the goal is to know each other's, not to be identical.

5. Budget

The book reframes "budget" as freedom, not restriction - it keeps you out of debt and shows you clearly what's coming in and going out. A good rule of thumb it offers: tithe 10%, save 10%, and set aside a little to invest in your marriage (date nights, books, getaways).

6. Together before our next session

After you've both finished Parts A and B on your own, sit together for 20 minutes, ideally over a meal or coffee, no phones.

  1. Swap your gift answers (Part A). Read aloud the gift you named in each other and the moment you saw it. Receive it - don't deflect it.
  2. Compare your finance answers (Part B). Especially: debts, tithing, who dispenses what, and your 5-year goal. Notice where you align and where you differ - don't try to solve it all tonight.
  3. Write one sentence together finishing this prompt:
    "The thing we most need to get on the same page about with money is ____."

Don't try to resolve everything - that's what the next session is for.
